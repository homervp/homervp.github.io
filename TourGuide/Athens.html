Homer's Tour Guide to Athens

An informal list of map pins and collected experiences

Museums

- Acropolis Museum

One of the top destinations in Athens, the building is impressive and the findings internally are some of the most important. Not the best museum I have seen, but a superb execution. The restaurant within is also considered quite good, whether for food or a drink.

- Benaki museum (folk)

Rather famous folk museum, i.e. with traditional dresses and life etc. Haven't been there since school time (when I maybe went a few too many times)

- National Historical Museum (Old Parliament)

More recent history (that I remember), including more on the War of Independence (1821-1832)

- Numismatic (judging by Corfu’s)

(going entirely by my experience in Corfu) should be showing the history of Greece from the 1800s onwards through its reflection in the changes in the design of coins and bank notes.

- Of Cycladic Art

Famous, but Iam not that drawn to the artstyle of the Cycladic civilization. To my shame, I have not bothered going. Maybe I go this week.

Archaeological sites

- Acropolis (of course)

Including the following:

o Parthenon

The third one, actually. First was destroyed by the Persians, second was still in construction the second time the persians came around and its foundations were reused and the base of the pillars are visible on the wall of the Acropolis. The third one is the one standing, visual tricks were used like curving the base and the outline of the pilars to correct for, or correct, optical illusions.

o Gates of Hermes

o Heraion

o Erechthion

- Odeion of Herod of Attica

You can watch live musical, opera and theater performances there, often of very big international names. Definitely worth it.

- Ancient Agora

- Roman Agora

- Arch of Hadrian

I keep forgetting this exists because I'm rarely then.

- Columns of Olympian Zeus

- Filopappou hill

Have to double-check accessibility - but the sunset should be great. There are tidbits of historical spots all over:

o Socrates’ prison

o Pnika

Assembly point of the people of Athens to discuss and vote on big issues.

- Lyceum of Aristotle

Place where Aristotle lectured, known from descriptions.

- Theater of Dionysus Eleutherus

To my disgrace, I didn't even know this existed until earlier this year. To my countrymens' disgrace, it's because I don't think anyone ever mentioned it. Apparently the oldest (surviving?) theater (to Dionysus). Also, it could hold 25.000 people!

- Areopagus / Areios Pagos

Point of judgement of the highest court cases in ancient Athens. The modern day high court takes its name from this spot.

Historical places

- Armored Cruiser Averof

Often mis-translated as Battleship Averof in Greek, it is definitely a Protected Cruiser, later known as Armored Cruiser (sorry, pet peeve). Italian-built ship ordered by Greece in the 1900s, flagship during the Balkan Wars and First World War, survived the second, formally still the flagship, and now a museum ship.

o Trireme Olympias (?)

Faithful reproduction of an ancient Greek trireme, was sailed from Crete to Athens before the 2004 Olympics (and my father was captaining the naval escort ship, which totally didn't bias my inclusion here). Note that I do not know if it is visitable, it was behind a fence last time, but it's next to the Averof.

o Floating Museum “Neraida”

Never been there actually.

- Parliament (Syntagma square)

There is a change of guard in the formal traditional military uniform every hour. Also a popular place for Greeks to throw moutzes at. Also the former palace when we had a king.

- Monastiraki Square

Frequent meeting point right in the middle of various places.

o Hadrian’s Library

Not visitable, that I know of, but very hard to miss.

- Zappeion garden / National Garden

It's a park. Potentially the only park in Greece that's on the level with those of European parks.

Culture

- Gazi & Technopolis (exhibitions, music festivals)

Hotspot of nightlife and in the middle of a lot of cultural activity, accessible directly by metro.

- Theatro Vrachon (Melina Merkouri Theater)

This is far, but is an absolutely unique theater, located under a cliff face.

- Megaron / Athens Concert Hall / Megaro Mousikis

Biggest concert hall in Athens, pretty good one too. Higher class.

- Thiseion Open Air Cinema

Has won some international awards, very conveniently situated in the heart of Athens. Open-air cinemas are probably all closed at this time of year but they are quite great.

Areas

- Kolonaki

Old high-class area with nice cafés and shops

- Kerameikos/Gazi

Hotspot of nightlife and culture. The old gas plant hosts cultural events, from exhibitions to concerts.

- Plaka

One of the oldest neighborhoods in Athens. Many nice little cafés and taverns. I like the ones that are on the stairs between the streets the most. Aim for the ones that do not have someone inviting you in :)

- Jacqueline de Romilly square – drinks/food with a view (also the rooftops on the side)

View toward the Acropolis. Many of the shops have rooftop gardens or balconies that might offer a better view.

Food

- Lukumades

(Name of the shop but also the sweet) Traditional Greek sweet, balls of dough, deep-fried, traditionally served with honey+cinnamon or with powdered sugar. Modern takes also use chocolate and fillings.

- Romanakia (far)

Impractically far unless you live in the area, but a vetted and favourite Cretan restaurant. Also saw the most famous Greek rock singer there.

- Tzitzikas & Mermigkas (chain)

Chain of restaurants with great dishes, many are likely fusion or contemporary Greek cuisine, but very good nonetheless.

- President Hotel rooftop

Great anoramic view. Good quality but location is the key attractor.

- Bairaktaris (Monastiraki or Syntagma square)

Famous but I will not say that it is all that exceptional. The Monastiraki branch spans, I believe, more than one block, and often features live music. The Syntagma branch is a smaller shop with good gyros.

- Nancy’s Sweet Home / Serbetospito of Nancy

Amazing sweets, though maybe not particularly traditional. Close to Monastiraki.

- Konstantinidis pastry shops

Chain of pastry shops with a mix of traditional and contemporary sweets, well-earned reputation.

- Terkenlis pastry shops

Chain of pastry shops with more contemporary sweets. The chocolate-covered tsoureki (Greek bread, sort of brioche-like but not really) is their signature

- Klimataria

Good food but more so nice environment.

Day Trips

- Aegina (40 mins from port, ~2h overhead each way including metro/waiting)

I would actually recommend it more as a weekend, but if you want a taste of the islands, it's also good. Peanuts are known as "Aegina nuts" in Greek, as most of the production is on this island. The Aiakeion pastry shop has great pastries with peanuts, many being unique local creations. To the south is the rather pretty (at least by the seaside) settlement of Perdika, from where you can get a boat to the island of Moni (meaning monastery), where the ridiculously tame deer and peacoks regularly approach the edge of the beach in spite of the many tourists. There is a large temple to the east but I would deprioritize it. I would also deprioritize the large monastery on the way to the temple.

- Aerchaeological site of Sounio

Rather impressive temple of Apolo, also important site in myth (king Aegeas jumped off there when he saw that the ship of his son Theseus was sailing back with the sails signifying defeat), there are also ruins of an ancient shipyard (or is it dock?). Can be combined with other seaside locations for a nice lunch. An organized tour might not be the worst idea.

- Corinth (ancient Corinth + Akrocorinth) (1h by car)

There are two sites, ancient Corinth is an extensive archaeological site, featuring an agora and a theater, next to the archaeological museum. The other is the Akrocorinth, the acropolis of Corinth, up on the mountain overlooking it (haven't been there).

- Hydra

An island about 1.25 hours from Piraeus by Flying Dolphin (fast type of hydrophoil ship), wheeled vehicles (including motor vehicles) are banned from it, has some history especially with the war of independence, nice island in general and very small.

- Nafplion

First capital of Greece, actually one of the nicer towns, with a coherent style and classic architecture (ask me about my opinions on Greek architecture post-WWII... if you dare)

- Mykines / Mycinae

On the way to Nafplion, seat of Agamemnon (whose death mask and armor we actually have, Christopher), impressive archaeological site.

- Delphi

Legendary location of the great Oracle, which gave near-useless prophesies which, depending on interpretation, sometimes changed history. Like when it told the Athenian delegation that wooden walls would save them from the Persians, and while some started building wooden walls around the Acropolis, others interpreted it as meaning ships, which is how they actually defeated them.

Sorry, tangent. Anyway, great location, extensive arcaheological site (possibly one of the biggest uninterupted sites from classical antiquity)
